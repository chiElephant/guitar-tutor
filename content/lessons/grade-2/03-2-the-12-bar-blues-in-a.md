The 12-bar blues is the most played chord progression in the world. Learn it in A and you can jam with almost anyone. It uses last lesson's three seventh chords and a new rhythm: the shuffle.

```chords
A7 D7 E7
```

## The progression

Twelve bars, three chords. Count each bar as four beats:

```
A7 | A7 | A7 | A7 |
D7 | D7 | A7 | A7 |
E7 | D7 | A7 | E7 |
```

Then go straight back to the top. The final E7 is called the turnaround: it pushes you into the next twelve bars. Play it round four times with D DU UDU before you touch the shuffle.

## The shuffle

A shuffle is a bouncing rhythm: long-short, long-short, like "hump-ty dump-ty". Instead of strumming the whole chord, you play two strings at a time on the bass side. For A7 the pattern is open A string together with the D string at fret 2, then open A with the D string at fret 4:

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.5 2.4).8 (0.5 2.4).8 (0.5 4.4).8 (0.5 4.4).8 (0.5 2.4).8 (0.5 2.4).8 (0.5 4.4).8 (0.5 4.4).8 | (0.1 2.2 0.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 0.3 2.4 0.5).8 (0.1 2.2 0.3).8 r.8 (0.1 2.2 0.3).8 (0.1 2.2 0.3 2.4 0.5).8 (0.1 2.2 0.3).8 |
```

Bar one is the A7 shuffle written as straight eighths; bar two is A7 strummed for comparison. Downstrokes throughout. Play the written eighths **swung**: lean on the first of each pair and make the second one late and light. The player's straight version is for reading; your ears give you the swing.

Use finger 1 for fret 2 and finger 3 for fret 4. For the D7 bars, move the same pattern over one string: open D with the G string at frets 2 and 4. For E7, drop down one string: open low E with the A string at frets 2 and 4.

## Keeping your place

Beginners lose count in bars 7 and 11, where A7 comes back early. Say the bar numbers aloud: "one two three four, D7 five six, A7 seven eight, E9, D10, A11, E12."

## Practice

- The progression with D DU UDU, 70 bpm, four times round.
- The A7 shuffle bar alone, 70 bpm, until the swing feels natural.
- The whole 12 bars as a shuffle, 60 bpm, saying the bar numbers.
- Record yourself and play the A minor pentatonic notes from module 7 over it once you get there.
