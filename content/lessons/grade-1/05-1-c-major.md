C major is the last big open chord of the grade and the one most beginners find hardest to make ring. It is also the chord that unlocks Amazing Grace, Auld Lang Syne and almost every folk song in G. Go slowly and give your fingers a week.

```chords
C
```

## Placing the fingers

1. **First finger** on string 2 (B) at fret 1.
2. **Second finger** on string 4 (D) at fret 2.
3. **Third finger** on string 5 (A) at fret 3.
4. Strings 3 and 1 open. Strum from string 5; **skip string 6.**

Look at the shape: it is a diagonal staircase climbing towards the thick strings. The third finger has the longest reach, and it is the finger that most often leans and mutes string 4. Arch it.

## From Am to C

Hold Am. Fingers 1 and 2 are already in their C positions. Only finger 3 moves, from string 3 fret 2 to string 5 fret 3. So Am to C is a one-finger change. Practise it that way: keep fingers 1 and 2 planted and just hop finger 3.

## From C to G

This is the change you will play most for the rest of your life. If you learned G with fingers 2, 3 and 4, the move is: finger 2 goes from string 4 to string 5 (same fret), finger 3 goes from string 5 fret 3 to string 6 fret 3, and finger 4 reaches down to string 1 fret 3. Finger 1 lifts off. Both chords lean the same way, so the hand barely rotates.

## Practice

- Place C, pick each of the five strings, fix dead ones, repeat ten times.
- One-minute changes Am to C, then C to G. Log both.
- C | G | C | G with four down-strums, 60 bpm.
