Old Joe Clark is an American fiddle tune with a strange, bright sound. That sound comes from the two chords underneath it: A and G, a pair that does not usually belong together. You know both already.

Listen: [Play Old Joe Clark](piece:old-joe-clark).

## The progression

Both halves of the tune use the same accompaniment:

```
A | A | A | G |
A | A | G | A |
```

Play each half twice. The G is the surprise; make it count by changing to it right on beat 1.

## The A to G change

This is a big move. Every finger changes string and fret, and G is a stretch. Break it down:

1. From A, lift all three fingers together, keeping their shape.
2. Finger 2 lands on string 5 fret 2 first. Think of it as the anchor.
3. Fingers 3 and 4 reach out to strings 6 and 1 at fret 3.

Practise the change silently, no strumming, just placing and lifting, until the fingers go to the right spots without looking. Then add the strum.

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 | (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 |
```

## Strumming for a fast tune

The recording may feel quick. Rhythm players on fast tunes often strum once per beat but let the strum be light and short. Keep the pick moving in a small arc, close to the strings. If four strums per bar is too much, play on beats 1 and 3 only. The tune still works.

## Practice

- One-minute changes A to G. Log it.
- Old Joe Clark at 60 bpm, two strums per bar, both halves.
- Old Joe Clark with the recording, one strum per bar, listening for the G bars.
- Play Soldier's Joy from last module once to keep it fresh.
