Guitar players share music in two simple pictures: **chord boxes** for chords and **tab** for melodies. Neither needs you to read standard notation. You will use both in every lesson from here on.

## Chord boxes

A chord box is the neck seen from the front with the headstock at the top. The six vertical lines are the strings, thick low E on the left and thin high e on the right. Horizontal lines are frets. A dot shows where a finger goes; the number in or under the dot is the finger: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 little. An **O** above a string means play it open. An **X** means do not play it.

```chords
Em
```

Read this box: strings 6, 3, 2 and 1 open; finger 2 on string 5 fret 2; finger 3 on string 4 fret 2. That is E minor, your first chord next lesson.

## Tab

Tab has six lines too, but here the **top line is the thin high e string** and the bottom line is the thick low E. Numbers are frets: 0 means open, 3 means fret 3. Read left to right. Numbers stacked on top of each other are played together.

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 0.1.4 2.1.4 3.1.4 0.2.4 | (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).2 r.2 |
```

Bar one: four single notes on strings 1 and 2. Bar two: all six strings at once, which is the Em chord from the box, held for two beats, then two beats of silence. The notation above the tab shows the rhythm; the tab shows where your fingers go.

A tip: the app highlights each note as it plays. Watch the cursor the first few times rather than trying to count.

## Practice

- Play the single-note bar above three times, slowly. Say "open, two, three, open".
- Cover the tab and play it from memory.
- Find Em on the chord box, place your fingers, strum once. Check each string rings.
