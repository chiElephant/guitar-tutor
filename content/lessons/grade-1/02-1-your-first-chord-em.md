E minor is the friendliest chord on the guitar: two fingers, four open strings, and all six strings ring. It sounds moody and full. It is also the shape you will build other chords from.

```chords
Em
```

## Placing the fingers

1. Put your **second finger** on string 5 (the A string) at fret 2.
2. Put your **third finger** on string 4 (the D string) at fret 2, right next to it.
3. Keep your thumb on the back of the neck, roughly behind those fingers.

Press with the fingertips, just behind the fret wire. Curl the fingers so they stand up like little arches and do not lean on the strings next door. Strings 6, 3, 2 and 1 are open.

## Check each string

Before you strum, pick each string one at a time from thick to thin. All six should ring clearly. A dull "thunk" means a finger is touching a neighbouring string, or you are not pressing enough, or you are too far from the fret. Fix it one string at a time.

## The down-strum

Hold the pick loosely. Brush down across all six strings in one easy motion, starting from the thick string. Think of flicking water off your hand rather than dragging a brush through paint. Four even down-strums make one bar:

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 |
```

Count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud and strum on every number. Keep the strums the same size and the same volume.

## Practice

- Place Em, pick each string to check it, lift off, repeat ten times.
- Strum four down-strums per bar for one minute, counting aloud.
- Close your eyes, place Em by feel, then strum. Open your eyes and check.
