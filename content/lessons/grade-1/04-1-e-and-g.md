Two more chords and you will know six. E major is the big, ringing chord of blues and rock; G major is the home chord of most folk songs. Both use all six strings, so no aiming needed: strum everything.

```chords
E G
```

## E major

Hold Am. Now move the whole shape one string towards the ceiling, so every finger lands on the next thicker string. Finger 1 on string 3 fret 1, finger 2 on string 5 fret 2, finger 3 on string 4 fret 2. Strings 6, 2 and 1 open. The hand shape is identical to Am; only the strings change. That is why the Am to E change is one of the quickest you will learn.

## G major

1. **Second finger** on string 5 (A) at fret 2.
2. **Third finger** on string 6 (low E) at fret 3.
3. **Fourth finger** (little finger) on string 1 (high e) at fret 3.
4. Strings 4, 3 and 2 open.

G is a stretch at first. Many players use fingers 1, 2 and 3 instead of 2, 3 and 4. Both are correct. The 2-3-4 version makes the change to C (next module) much easier, so give it a fair try before you decide.

## Two useful changes

- **Am to E**: keep the shape, shift it across one string. Fingers stay in the same arrangement.
- **Em to G**: finger 2 is already on string 5 fret 2 in Em. Leave it there. Lift finger 3 to string 6 fret 3 and add finger 4 on string 1 fret 3.

## Practice

- One-minute changes Am to E. Log it.
- One-minute changes Em to G. Log it.
- E | Am | E | Am and G | Em | G | Em, four down-strums per bar, 60 bpm.
