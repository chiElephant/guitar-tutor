Before you play a note, spend ten minutes getting comfortable with the instrument. Good hand and body position from day one makes everything later easier.

## The parts you need to know

- **Headstock and tuning pegs** at the top. Turning a peg changes the pitch of one string.
- **Nut**: the small bar the strings pass over before the fretboard.
- **Frets**: the metal strips along the neck. "Fret 1" is the space between the nut and the first strip. You press the string just behind a strip, not on top of it.
- **Body and soundhole** (or pickups on an electric): where you strum.

## The six strings

The thickest string is nearest your face. We number strings from the floor up: **string 1 is the thinnest (high e)** and **string 6 is the thickest (low E)**. From thick to thin the open notes are E A D G B e.

## Sitting

Sit on a chair without arms, feet flat. Rest the waist of the guitar on your right thigh (left thigh if you play left-handed). Keep the neck tilted slightly upward and the back of the guitar resting against your stomach, not lying flat on your lap. If you tip the guitar back to look at your fingers, your wrist pays for it.

## Your hands

Fretting hand: thumb roughly behind the second finger, on the back of the neck. Fingers curl so the tips land on the strings. Keep the wrist fairly straight.

Strumming hand: hold a medium pick between thumb and first finger with only a few millimetres showing.

Press play: each open string, thick to thin.

## Practice

- Pick up the guitar, sit, and set your posture three times. Notice what you adjust.
- Play each open string one at a time with the pick, counting "1, 2, 3, 4" per string.
- Say the string names E A D G B e out loud while you play them.
