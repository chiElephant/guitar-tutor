Until now every strum went down. Adding the up-strum doubles your vocabulary and gives you the pattern that drives most strummed songs: **D DU UDU**.

## The up-strum

Your arm is already coming back up after each down-strum. An up-strum simply lets the pick touch the strings on the way. Brush up across the thin strings only, roughly strings 1 to 3. Up-strums are lighter and quieter than down-strums; do not try to hit all six.

Count "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and". Down on the numbers, up on the "ands":

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 |
```

That is "D U D U D U D U", eight strums per bar. Play it until it is even.

## D DU UDU

Now remove two of those strums. The pattern is spoken as **"Down, Down-Up, Up-Down-Up"**:

| Beat | 1 | and | 2 | and | 3 | and | 4 | and |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strum | D | – | D | U | – | U | D | U |

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 r.8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).8 (0.1 0.2 0.3).8 |
```

The hard part is beat 3: your arm swings down but **misses** the strings, then catches them on the way up. Remember the air strum from last lesson. Keep the arm moving in a steady down-up motion the whole bar; the pattern is only about which swings make contact.

Say it out loud before you play it. "Down. Down up. Up down up." Then say it while you play, slowly, at 50 bpm.

## Practice

- D U D U D U D U on Em for one minute, even and light.
- D DU UDU on Em at 50 bpm for two minutes, saying the pattern aloud.
- D DU UDU on Em | Am, one bar each, changing on the final up-strum.
