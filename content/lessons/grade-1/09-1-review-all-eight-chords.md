Before the checkpoint, take stock. You know eight chords, two strumming patterns, a riff, a melody and the capo. This lesson is a tune-up for the whole set, aimed at the changes that still slow you down.

```chords
Em Am D A E G C Dm
```

## The chord audit

Play each chord once, then pick through its strings. For every chord, answer three questions: Does every string ring? Can I place it without looking? Can I place it in one movement rather than finger by finger? Write a tick or a cross next to each chord in your log. Crosses tell you what to practise today.

## The change grid

Here are the changes used by the songs in this grade. Run one-minute changes on each and log the number:

| Change | Used in |
|---|---|
| Em – Am | module 2 vamp |
| D – A | Soldier's Joy |
| A – G | Old Joe Clark |
| C – G, G – D | Amazing Grace |
| Am – G, Am – C | Scarborough Fair |
| G – D, Em – C, Am – D | Auld Lang Syne, The Water Is Wide |
| Am – E | Greensleeves |
| Dm – C | Shady Grove |

Any change below 30 per minute is the one that will trip you in a song. Spend five minutes on your lowest score before anything else.

## Strum check

Play this four-chord loop with D DU UDU at 60 bpm, one bar each:

G | Em | C | D. If the strumming arm stops when a change is late, that is the habit to break: keep the arm moving and let the chord arrive when it arrives.
