An out-of-tune guitar makes every chord sound wrong, no matter how well you play it. Tune every single time you pick up the instrument. It takes two minutes and it trains your ear.

## Target notes

| String | Note |
|---|---|
| 6 (thickest) | E |
| 5 | A |
| 4 | D |
| 3 | G |
| 2 | B |
| 1 (thinnest) | e |

## With the tuner

Open the app's tuner, play one string, and let it ring. The display shows the nearest note and whether you are flat (too low) or sharp (too high). Turn the peg for that string slowly. If the pitch goes the wrong way, turn the other way. Tune *up* to the note: if you are sharp, drop below the target and come back up. Strings hold pitch better that way.

Play the string again after you stop turning. Pegs settle.

## By ear, using the guitar itself

You can tune five strings from one. Fret string 6 at fret 5: that note is A, the same as open string 5. Play both and adjust string 5 until they match. Repeat down the neck:

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 5.6.4 0.5.4 5.5.4 0.4.4 | 5.4.4 0.3.4 4.3.4 0.2.4 | 5.2.4 0.1.4 r.2 |
```

Notice the odd one: string 3 is fretted at **fret 4**, not 5, to match string 2. When two notes are close but not matching you hear a slow "wobble". As they come into tune the wobble slows down and disappears.

## Practice

- Tune with the tuner, then detune one string a little and bring it back by ear using the fret-5 method.
- Hum each open string before you play it, then check yourself.
- Tune at the start of every practice session this week without being reminded.
