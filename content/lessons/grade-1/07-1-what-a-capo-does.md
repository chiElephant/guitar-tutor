A capo is a clamp that holds down all six strings at one fret. It turns that fret into a new nut, so every open chord you know comes out higher. It is how most guitarists match a song to a singer's voice.

## Putting it on

Clamp it just behind the fret wire, straight across the neck. If a string buzzes, the capo is too far from the fret or too loose. Retune after clamping.

## What happens to your chords

Each fret is one semitone. Capo on fret 1 raises everything by one semitone, fret 2 by two, and so on. Your fingers play the same shapes; the sounds move up.

| Shape you play | Capo 1 | Capo 2 | Capo 3 | Capo 4 | Capo 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | G# | A | Bb | B | C |
| C | C# | D | Eb | E | F |
| D | Eb | E | F | F# | G |
| Em | Fm | F#m | Gm | G#m | Am |
| Am | Bbm | Bm | Cm | C#m | Dm |

With the capo on fret 2, the **G shape sounds like A** and the **C shape sounds like D**. Try it: capo on fret 2, play the G shape; capo off, play A. Same chord, two fingerings.

```chords
G C D
```

## The two ways people use it

1. **To match a voice.** Song too low to sing? Move the capo up a fret at a time. The shapes never change.
2. **For a brighter sound.** The same chords at fret 3 or 4 ring lighter and more jangly. Two guitarists, one open and one capoed, sound wonderful together.

## Practice

- Clamp the capo at fret 2, tune, and play Em | Am | D | A with D DU UDU.
- Move it to fret 4 and play the same progression. Listen to how the mood lifts.
- Capo 2, play the G shape; capo off, play A. Repeat until you hear they match.
