Knowing a chord is one skill. Getting to it in time is another. From now on, every pair of chords gets the **one-minute change** drill. It is the single most useful exercise in Grade 1.

## The drill

1. Set a timer for 60 seconds.
2. Strum Em once, change to Am, strum once, change back. Keep going.
3. Count how many changes you make in the minute. Write the number in your practice log.

Do not rush. A change only counts if the chord rings cleanly. Speed comes on its own when the movement becomes automatic. Most people start somewhere between 10 and 20; a comfortable target is 40 or more.

## Make the change smaller

Watch your fingers while you change from Em to Am. Fingers 2 and 3 slide across from strings 5–4 to strings 4–3 at the same fret. Keep them low; do not lift them far from the strings. Finger 1 lands on string 2 fret 1 at the same moment. Think of the three fingers moving as one unit.

## Your first song

Here is a two-chord progression you can play right now. Each chord gets one bar of four down-strums:

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 | (0.1 1.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 1.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 1.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 1.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 |
```

Progression: **Em | Am | Em | Am** and repeat. Play it with the metronome at 60 bpm. Count "1, 2, 3, 4" and change on the "and" before the next "1". If the change is late, leave out strum 4 of the bar to buy time; a clean chord with a gap beats a messy chord on time.

Listen to [Play Scarborough Fair](piece:scarborough-fair) for how an Am-based tune sounds. You will play its chords in module 5.

## Practice

- One-minute changes Em to Am. Log your score.
- Em | Am | Em | Am at 60 bpm for two minutes with four down-strums per bar.
- Same progression with one strum per bar, changing early and silently.
