D and A are the two chords behind hundreds of folk and fiddle tunes. Both are "major" chords, so they sound bright next to the minor chords you already know. Both skip the thickest strings, so the strumming-hand aim you practised on Am matters here too.

```chords
D A
```

## D major

1. **First finger** on string 3 (G) at fret 2.
2. **Second finger** on string 1 (high e) at fret 2.
3. **Third finger** on string 2 (B) at fret 3.
4. String 4 (D) is open. Strum from string 4 down; **skip strings 6 and 5.**

The shape is a small triangle. The third finger sits a fret higher than the other two. Keep all three fingers on their tips; the first finger loves to lean over and kill string 2.

## A major

1. **First finger** on string 4 (D) at fret 2.
2. **Second finger** on string 3 (G) at fret 2.
3. **Third finger** on string 2 (B) at fret 2.
4. Strings 5 and 1 open. Strum from string 5; **skip string 6.**

Three fingers in a row at the same fret is a tight squeeze. Angle the fingers slightly so the tips line up, and let the first finger sit a touch behind the others. If it is simply too crowded, some players use fingers 2, 3 and 4 instead; try both and keep whichever rings cleanest.

## The D to A change

The first finger stays at fret 2 and simply moves from string 3 to string 4. Make that finger the anchor: move it first, then let fingers 2 and 3 fall into their new spots.

## Practice

- Place D, pick strings 4 to 1 to check them, release, repeat ten times. Same for A.
- One-minute changes D to A. Log the score.
- D | A | D | A with four down-strums per bar at 60 bpm.
