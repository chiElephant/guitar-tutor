You have strummed chords and played a riff. Now play a melody that everyone recognises: the theme from the last movement of Beethoven's Ninth Symphony, written in 1824 and long out of copyright. It sits on the two thinnest strings and uses only frets 0, 1 and 3.

Open it here: [Play Ode to Joy](piece:ode-to-joy). Press play and watch the cursor move through the tab once before you pick anything up.

## The first phrase

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 0.1.4 0.1.4 1.1.4 3.1.4 | 3.1.4 1.1.4 0.1.4 3.2.4 | 1.2.4 1.2.4 3.2.4 0.1.4 | 0.1.4 3.2.4 3.2.2 |
```

String 1 open is the note E; fret 1 is F; fret 3 is G. On string 2, fret 3 is D and fret 1 is C. So the melody only uses five notes: C D E F G. Say the fret numbers aloud as you play.

## Fingering

Use one finger per fret: finger 1 for fret 1, finger 3 for fret 3. Do not slide one finger around. This "one finger per fret" habit is the basis of every scale you will learn.

## Let the notes ring

For melodies, press and hold each note until the next one starts. Lift too early and you get a gap; lift too late and two notes blur together. Listen to the recording's smoothness and copy it.

## Rhythm

Almost every note is one beat, so this is a great piece for counting. The last bar of the phrase has a longer note; hold it for two beats and let it fade. The full piece in the library has one dotted rhythm; the player will show it to you.

## Practice

- First phrase at 60 bpm, downstrokes, three times.
- Same phrase with the metronome off, listening for even spacing.
- Learn the second phrase from the library piece; it is nearly identical.
- Play the whole theme along with the player at 60 bpm.
