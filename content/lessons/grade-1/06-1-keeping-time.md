A listener forgives a wrong chord long before they forgive a wobbly beat. This module is about your strumming hand, and it starts with the click.

## Why the metronome

When you practise alone, you slow down for hard changes and speed up for easy ones without noticing. The metronome does not. It tells you the truth about where you lose time, and that is exactly where to practise.

Open the app's metronome and set 60 bpm. Clap with it for thirty seconds. Then tap your foot with it. Then strum muted strings (rest your fretting hand lightly across all six strings so they make a "chk" rather than a note) with it. Only then add a chord.

## Down-strums on the beat

Every click is a down-strum. Your arm should move down *through* the click, not land on it. Think of the strum as a swing that passes the strings at the click, with the arm already on its way back up by the time the sound fades.

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 | (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 r.4 (0.1 0.2 0.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).4 r.4 |
```

Bar one: four strums. Bar two: strum on 1 and 3 only, and keep the arm swinging on 2 and 4 without touching the strings. That "air strum" is the secret to every pattern coming next. Your arm never stops; it just chooses when to make contact.

## Finding your tempo

The right practice tempo is the one where you can change chords without rushing. For most people in week four that is 50 to 70 bpm. Write it down. Each week try to raise it by 4 bpm, and go back down the moment the changes get sloppy.

## Practice

- Clap, tap and mute-strum with the click at 60 bpm, 30 seconds each.
- Em for one minute: four strums per bar, arm swinging constantly.
- Em | Am | D | A, one bar each, strum on 1 and 3 only, arm swinging on 2 and 4.
