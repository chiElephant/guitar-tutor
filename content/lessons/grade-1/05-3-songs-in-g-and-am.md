You now have enough chords to play two of the best-known melodies in the English-speaking world. Both are in **three time**: count "1, 2, 3" instead of "1, 2, 3, 4". That is the only new idea in this lesson.

## Three-time strumming

Three down-strums per bar, with the first a little heavier:

```alphatex
\ts 3 4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 | (0.1 1.2 0.3 2.4 3.5).4 (0.1 1.2 0.3 2.4 3.5).4 (0.1 1.2 0.3 2.4 3.5).4 |
```

Say "ONE two three, ONE two three" and let your strumming arm follow the words.

## Amazing Grace in G

Listen: [Play Amazing Grace](piece:amazing-grace). Chords, one per bar, sixteen bars:

```
G | G | C | G |
G | G | D | D |
G | G | C | G |
G | Em | D | G |
```

The C to G and G to D changes are the ones to drill. The Em in bar 14 is optional; play G there if the change is too much for now.

## Scarborough Fair in A minor

Listen: [Play Scarborough Fair](piece:scarborough-fair). A simple accompaniment:

```
Am | Am | G | Am |
Am | C | D | Am |
Am | G | C | Am |
G | Am | G | Am |
```

The Am to G change is new. Finger 2 stays on string 5 fret 2 for both chords; lift fingers 1 and 3 and reach fingers 3 and 4 out to fret 3. Keep finger 2 planted as the anchor.

## Practice

- One-minute changes G to D, then Am to G. Log both.
- Amazing Grace with the recording, one strum per bar, then three.
- Scarborough Fair with the recording, one strum per bar.
- Pick whichever song you like more and play it twice through with three strums per bar at 60 bpm.
