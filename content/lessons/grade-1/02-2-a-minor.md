A minor is your second chord and a close cousin of Em. It uses three fingers, leaves two strings open, and skips the thick low E string. Learn it carefully: the same three-finger shape comes back as E major and C major later.

```chords
Am
```

## Placing the fingers

1. **First finger** on string 2 (B) at fret 1.
2. **Second finger** on string 4 (D) at fret 2.
3. **Third finger** on string 3 (G) at fret 2, below the second finger.
4. String 5 (A) rings open. String 1 (high e) rings open.
5. **Do not strum string 6.** Start your down-strum from string 5.

Starting the strum from string 5 is the new skill. Aim the pick at the A string and let the motion carry through the rest. If you hit the low E by accident it will not sound terrible, but the chord loses its clean A-minor sound.

## Compare with Em

Hold Em, then Am. Notice the second and third fingers stay at fret 2 but hop from strings 5–4 to strings 4–3, and the first finger joins in on string 2. That shared shape is why we learn them together.

## Common problems

- **String 1 is muted**: your first finger is lying flat and touching it. Stand it up on its tip.
- **String 2 buzzes**: move the first finger closer to the fret wire.
- **The chord sounds thin**: you missed string 5 or hit string 6. Watch the pick.

## Practice

- Place Am, pick each of the five strings from A to high e, fix any dead string, repeat ten times.
- Four down-strums per bar on Am for one minute.
- Switch slowly between Em and Am, pausing as long as you like, ten times each way.
