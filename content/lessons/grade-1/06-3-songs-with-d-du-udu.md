Time to use the new pattern on real songs. Both songs below are in G and use four or five chords you know. Play D DU UDU once per bar unless the chord changes mid-bar.

## Auld Lang Syne in G

Listen: [Play Auld Lang Syne](piece:auld-lang-syne). Each line below is four bars:

```
G | D | Em | C |
G | D | C | G |
G | D | Em | C |
G | D | C | G |
```

The chord changes every bar, so your fretting hand has to be ready on the last "up" of each pattern. Start at 50 bpm. If a change is late, drop the final up-strum of the bar and use that time to move.

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).8 (3.1 0.2 0.3).8 r.8 (3.1 0.2 0.3).8 (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).8 (3.1 0.2 0.3).8 | (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).4 (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).8 (2.1 3.2 2.3).8 r.8 (2.1 3.2 2.3).8 (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).8 (2.1 3.2 2.3).8 |
```

That is the first two bars, G then D, with the pattern written out. Notice the up-strums only use the top three strings.

## The Water Is Wide in G

Listen: [Play The Water Is Wide](piece:the-water-is-wide). A gentle accompaniment:

```
G | C | G | G |
Em | Am | D | D |
G | C | G | Em |
Am | D | G | G |
```

This one has two bars in a row on the same chord in several places. Enjoy them: they are your breathing space. The Em to Am and Am to D changes are the new ones. Both share no fingers, so practise them silently first.

## A note on volume

With a full pattern it is tempting to hit everything hard. Instead, make the down-strum on beat 1 the loudest and let the rest sit underneath. The song will immediately sound more musical.

## Practice

- One-minute changes Em to Am and Am to D.
- Auld Lang Syne, D DU UDU, 50 bpm, all the way through.
- The Water Is Wide, D DU UDU, 50 bpm, all the way through.
- Play one of them with the recording and try to stay with the melody.
