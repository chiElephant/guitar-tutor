A riff is a short, repeating musical phrase. It is the part people hum from a rock song, usually single notes or power chords on the thick strings. Here is one written for this course, in E minor, using only frets 0 to 3 on strings 6 and 5.

## The riff

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 0.6.8 0.6.8 3.6.8 3.6.8 0.6.8 0.6.8 2.5.8 3.5.8 | 0.6.8 0.6.8 3.6.8 3.6.8 0.6.8 0.6.8 3.5.8 2.5.8 |
```

Read the tab: the bottom line is string 6. Open, open, fret 3, fret 3, open, open, then two notes on string 5. The second bar is the same except the last two notes swap. Play it round and round.

## Fingering

- Use finger 3 for fret 3 and finger 2 for fret 2. Keep finger 1 hovering over fret 1 even though it never plays; that keeps your hand in position.
- Press just behind the fret wire, as with chords, but you only need one finger down at a time. Let the others relax.

## Picking

Play every note with a **downstroke** first. Once the notes are clean, try **alternate picking**: down on the numbered beats, up on the "ands". It feels odd at first, but it is how you will eventually play fast. Keep the pick close to the string.

## Lock in with the beat

Set the metronome to 60 bpm. Each click is two notes. Say "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and" and make sure the open strings land on the numbers. Riffs live or die on timing. A slightly wrong note in time sounds better than a right note late.

## Make it yours

Add a power chord on the final note: play (0.6 2.5) instead of the single note. Or hold the last note of bar 2 for a whole beat. Small changes like these are how riffs become songs.

## Practice

- The riff at 60 bpm, downstrokes only, for two minutes.
- The riff at 60 bpm with alternate picking.
- Loop bar 1 only until it is completely even, then add bar 2.
- Play the riff four times, then E5 | A5 for two bars, then the riff again.
