Greensleeves is a sixteenth-century English tune, and its chords are a lovely workout for Am, G, E and C. Then we move it with the capo so you can hear the same song in a new key.

Listen: [Play Greensleeves](piece:greensleeves). The tune is in a lilting three or six feel; count "1, 2, 3" and use three down-strums per bar, or one on the first beat and a lighter one on the third.

## The progression, no capo

```
Am | G | Am | E |
Am | G | Am  E | Am |
C | G | Am | E |
C | G | Am  E | Am |
```

Where two chords share a bar, give each one strum (beats 1 and 3, or 1 and 2 then rest). The **Am to E** change is the one to drill: same finger shape, moved across one string.

## Now with the capo

Put the capo on fret 3 and play exactly the same shapes. The song is now in C minor: your Am shape sounds as Cm, G as Bb, E as G, C as Eb. You could not play those chords any other way at this stage, yet here they are, ringing out. That is the power of the capo.

Try fret 5 as well. Am shape becomes Dm, and the whole song gets a thinner, older sound. Pick the fret you like best and note it in your log.

## Practice

- One-minute changes Am to E. Log it.
- Greensleeves without capo, three strums per bar, 60 bpm.
- Greensleeves with capo at fret 3, same tempo.
- Play along with the recording at whichever fret matches it best (hint: no capo).
