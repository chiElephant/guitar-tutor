Soldier's Joy is one of the oldest fiddle tunes still played, and its chords are just D and A. It is a perfect first real song: fast enough to be fun, simple enough to play in week two.

Listen first: [Play Soldier's Joy](piece:soldiers-joy). You are not playing the melody yet; you are the rhythm guitarist behind it.

## The progression

The tune has two eight-bar halves. A simple accompaniment for each half is:

```
D | D | D | A |
D | D | A | D |
```

Repeat each half, then play the whole thing again. That is the standard "AABB" shape of a fiddle tune.

## How to practise it

Start with one down-strum per bar. Say the chord name on beat 1 of each bar and change on beat 4 of the bar before. Once that is steady, move to two strums per bar (beats 1 and 3), then four. Only increase when the chords still ring clean.

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).4 (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).4 (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).4 (2.1 3.2 2.3 0.4).4 | (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 (0.1 2.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).4 |
```

The bar marked A is the one place you change. Listen for it in the recording: the harmony lifts a little on the A chord and settles when D returns.

## Playing along

Turn on the loop in the player and set the tempo to 60 bpm. Strum along with the recording. If you lose your place, stop strumming, listen for the start of the next eight bars, and come back in. Nobody plays through the whole thing clean the first day.

## Practice

- Say the progression aloud while tapping your foot, no guitar.
- Soldier's Joy at 60 bpm, one strum per bar, twice through.
- Soldier's Joy at 60 bpm, four strums per bar, once through. Note where you fell behind.
- One-minute changes D to A. Try to beat yesterday's score by two.
