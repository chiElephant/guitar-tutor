Five notes, six strings, one hand position. A minor pentatonic box 1 is the shape behind most blues and rock lead playing. You met it in Grade 2 as a fingering exercise; now learn it well enough to improvise with, because the rest of this module happens inside it.

## The shape

```fretboard
root: A
scale: minor pentatonic
frets: 5-8
```

Every string has two notes. On strings 6, 2 and 1 they are frets 5 and 8; on strings 5, 4 and 3 they are frets 5 and 7. The root note A is on string 6 fret 5, string 4 fret 7 and string 1 fret 5. Find all three first; phrases want to land on roots.

## Fingering

First finger covers fret 5 on every string. Third finger plays fret 7, fourth finger plays fret 8. Keep the hand still and let the fingers do the work. A weak fourth finger is normal and strengthens quickly.

## Up and down

```alphatex
\ts 4 4 5.6.8 8.6.8 5.5.8 7.5.8 5.4.8 7.4.8 5.3.8 7.3.8 | 5.2.8 8.2.8 5.1.8 8.1.8 8.1.8 5.1.8 8.2.8 5.2.8 | 7.3.8 5.3.8 7.4.8 5.4.8 7.5.8 5.5.8 8.6.8 5.6.8 |
```

Up from the low root to the high one and straight back down. Strict alternate picking, even across string changes. Set 60 bpm, two notes per click. Most people stumble at the top of the run; practise the string 2 to string 1 crossing on its own.

## The five notes

A C D E G. It skips the two notes of the natural minor that clash most easily, so anything you play in it over an A minor or A blues backing sounds acceptable. That safety lets you concentrate on rhythm and phrasing.

## First phrases

Play three notes, stop, breathe, play three more. End each phrase on a root. Then end one on C (string 6 fret 8 or string 2 fret 5) and hear how it wants to move on. Short phrases with space sound like music; a constant stream sounds like practice.

## Practice

- The run up and down at 60 bpm with alternate picking, five times, no mistakes.
- The same run with the metronome at 80 bpm.
- Three-note phrases over the 12-bar blues in A from Grade 2, ending on roots.
- Name the note aloud as you play each one, slowly.
